# Supplement C. Custody and signature attribution

The historical search study screened the full selection batch for overlap against a registered catalogue of old inputs. Its public request and receipt exposed aggregate coverage and a disposition. Row-level matches, source identities and the sealed input index were withheld from the proposer. The signed receipt records PASS.

The project used the same Windows account for execution and the PI's local signing arrangement. The public key, challenge and detached signature support a check that the registered key signed the relevant bytes. An operating-system access rule under the shared account does not establish independent control over signing. The overlap check therefore has procedural, shared-account status. Personal authorization for the delegated signature is disputed and has not been independently verified. The public receipt establishes the recorded decision under that arrangement. It cannot identify the person who invoked the key or prove independent custody of the material.

This limitation concerns the historical selection panel. Its evidential role is the comparison of the two search procedures on the same paid inputs and the observed stop at selection. The paired estimates, retained failures and stopping point retain their published scope.
