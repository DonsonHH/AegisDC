# Cover letter

Dear Editors of *Expert Systems with Applications*,

We submit for your consideration “Cost-aware value-of-information scheduling of inspections and interventions in maintenance-degraded computing facilities” as a research article.

An upstream sensor can show voltage while a backup path is disconnected. The decision facing the operator is whether to send someone to inspect the path, intervene immediately or preserve a time-limited permit for other work. Our expert system represents these choices through a finite belief over facility states, a public job calendar and a distinct record for verification of the work actually performed. The independent outcome is the service delivered while the crew is making and carrying out its choices.

A preregistered paired comparison across six synthetic families isolated access to a paid initial inspection. Mean normalized service deficit changed by −55.83 seconds, with a two-sided 95% interval of [−110.00, −11.61], at a higher labour cost. A separate post hoc comparison makes the complexity trade-off visible. A myopic rule matched the centre’s service on every reused development condition. A finite-budget tree planner used more labour and achieved more verified closures, with an unresolved difference in service deficit.

The paper connects interpretable online planning, constrained work scheduling and decision-oriented simulation, topics of direct interest to ESWA readers working on maintenance and inspection. The supplements locate the implementation versions and table entries, report the original qualification failure and correction, and document the evidence available for reproduction.
