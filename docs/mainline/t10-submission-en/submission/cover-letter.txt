Dear Editors of Expert Systems with Applications,

Please consider “When is a paid inspection worth waiting for during maintenance-induced degradation?” as a research article. The application is a coupled computing facility in which a decision-support policy must acquire evidence, decide whether to wait and dispatch work while permission and staff time are still available. Its knowledge is limited to records delivered by the decision time. The evaluator independently scores the service that the plant actually delivered.

Our central comparison changes one capability in an otherwise frozen policy. Across 144 paired synthetic conditions, allowing paid initial inspection reduced mean normalized service deficit by 55.83 s. A development case shows how a reading prevented an unnecessary write, while another shows how waiting delayed necessary work. These results identify an actionable design question for expert systems that can request information at a cost. The measured effect is a contrast per condition and includes occasions when the policy declined to buy a reading.

We retain the counterexamples and negative findings. The incident families support reductions in mean simulated severity. The event-rate endpoint was indeterminate. A selector developed on an earlier panel lost its advantage on new conditions. Ordinary and rewrite-capable search selected the same edit at equal budgets. Rewriting added no paid candidates, and an independent benefit from recursive self-improvement was not established. Labour exceeded the family tolerance in the Netherlands selection conditions. The study closed at FIT.

The enclosed manuscript describes the shared-account custody limitation. Its availability statement distinguishes public aggregates from sealed study records.
